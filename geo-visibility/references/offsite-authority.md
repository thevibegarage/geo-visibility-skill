# Off-site authority and corroboration

Engines are cautious: they prefer to recommend brands that several independent sources describe consistently. Large studies of AI citations find citations concentrate in a limited set of outlets and differ by engine, so the aim is presence in the specific sources your audit shows engines already use for your category.

## Build the target source list
From the Phase 1 audit, list every cited domain with: type, how often cited, which engines, whether the brand is present, whether competitors are present, effort to get in. Rank by (citation frequency x gap) / effort. Work the top 10 first.

## Source types and tactics
| Source type | What to do |
|---|---|
| Review platforms (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, app stores, Google reviews, industry-specific) | Claim and complete the profile (same fact sheet wording), add screenshots and pricing, run an ethical review-request program for real customers, respond to reviews. |
| "Best of" and comparison lists on publisher/affiliate sites | Identify which lists engines cite for your category, pitch factual inclusion with data, offer demo access and accurate specs, correct outdated entries. |
| Wikipedia / Wikidata | Only if the brand meets notability rules with independent coverage. Follow conflict-of-interest policy: propose edits on talk pages, do not self-edit promotional content. A Wikidata entry with accurate properties helps entity clarity. |
| Reddit, Quora, forums, Stack Overflow, Hacker News | Participate transparently with a real identity, disclose affiliation, answer questions with substance, do not astroturf. Monitor threads that engines cite and address legitimate criticism. |
| News and trade press | Digital PR around original data, benchmarks, and expert commentary; respond to journalist queries; announce real milestones. |
| Directories and databases (Crunchbase, LinkedIn, GitHub, industry associations, chambers, partner marketplaces) | Complete profiles with identical facts and `sameAs`-linked URLs. |
| YouTube and podcasts | Publish clear explainers with accurate titles, descriptions, chapters and transcripts; appear on relevant podcasts. Transcripts are often what gets retrieved. |
| Academic / standards / government | Contribute data to or get cited by authoritative bodies where relevant. |
| Partner and integration pages | Get listed on partners' marketplaces and docs with consistent descriptions. |

## Entity consistency
- Same legal and brand name, description, category terms, URL, logo everywhere.
- Fix legacy names and outdated facts (old pricing, former products, previous HQ) at the source; log them from the audit's inaccuracy list.
- Link official profiles both ways (site `sameAs` to profiles, profiles back to site).
- Founders and key people: consistent bios, real photos, linked profiles; expert authors build page-level trust.

## Reputation hygiene
- Monitor negative or inaccurate AI answers; trace each to its source and fix or respond there.
- Publish clear policies (refunds, privacy, security) so engines have accurate text to quote.
- Handle real criticism publicly and factually; engines summarize sentiment from what exists.

## Ethics guardrails
Do not: buy or fabricate reviews, create fake personas, mass-post undisclosed promotional comments, edit Wikipedia without disclosure, plant hidden instructions for AI agents on pages, or misstate facts. These violate platform rules, risk penalties and legal exposure, and tend to be filtered or backfire. Disclose affiliations when participating.

## 90-day sequencing
- Days 1-14: audit results, claim and complete top profiles, fix inaccuracies, fact sheet to all channels.
- Days 15-45: launch review program, pitch 5-10 list/comparison owners, publish first original-data asset, begin transparent community participation.
- Days 46-90: PR push around the data asset, expert commentary pipeline, partner listings, re-run the audit and compare share of voice.
