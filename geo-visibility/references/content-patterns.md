# Content patterns that get cited

AI engines extract passages, not pages. Write passages that survive being quoted out of context.

## Brand fact sheet (create first)
One source of truth the whole company uses. Fields:
- **One-sentence definition**: "[Brand] is a [category] that helps [audience] [outcome]." Same words everywhere.
- Category and subcategory (use the terms buyers actually use).
- Who it is for and who it is not for (exclusions build trust and improve match quality).
- 3-5 differentiators, each with a proof point (number, customer, certification, award).
- Pricing model and ranges (or "contact sales" stated plainly).
- Key facts: founded, HQ, team size, funding or ownership if public, customers, integrations.
- Official profiles (website, LinkedIn, GitHub, YouTube, Crunchbase, G2, Google Business Profile).
- Known competitors and honest comparison positioning.
- Approved boilerplate at 25, 50, and 100 words.
Mark missing items as TODO for the owner; never invent.

## Answer-first page pattern
```
H1: the question or topic in the buyer's words
Direct answer (40-80 words): self-contained, specific, no throat-clearing
Key facts box or table: numbers, specs, dates, pricing, who it's for
H2: sub-question 1 (phrased as a question)
  2-4 sentence answer first, then detail, then evidence
H2: sub-question 2 ...
Comparison table (if relevant): criteria as rows, options as columns, include competitors fairly
FAQ: 5-8 real questions with concise answers
Sources / methodology: link primary sources, state how data was gathered
Byline + credentials, published date, last updated date
```
Why: retrieval picks the best-matching passage; a heading that mirrors the prompt plus an immediate answer is the easiest passage to lift and quote.

## Page types worth building
1. **Definition / glossary** pages for category terms the brand should own.
2. **"Best X for Y" and "X alternatives"** pages: honest, criteria-based, includes competitors. These mirror the prompts buyers type and the list pages engines cite. Disclose bias; be fair or the page gets ignored.
3. **Comparison pages** ("Brand vs Competitor"): factual tables, where each wins, pricing as of a date.
4. **Pricing explainer**: plain numbers, what is included, typical total cost.
5. **Use-case / industry / persona pages**: specific scenarios with outcomes.
6. **Original research**: surveys, benchmarks, anonymized product data. The most citation-earning asset because others cite you in turn.
7. **How-to / implementation guides** written by practitioners with named authors.
8. **Customer proof**: case studies with real metrics (with permission).
9. **Docs and support content**: clear, crawlable, versioned. Agents rely on these.

## Writing rules
- Lead with the answer; context after.
- Be specific: numbers, dates, named entities, units. "Reduces onboarding from 14 days to 3 (2025 cohort of 212 teams)" beats "dramatically faster".
- One claim per sentence where possible; avoid pronouns that need earlier context.
- Define terms once, consistently; use the same product and brand names every time.
- Cite primary sources inline; link out generously to authoritative references. External citations to primary sources correlate with being cited.
- Keep opinion and fact distinguishable; attribute opinions.
- Include limitations and who the product is not for.
- Refresh time-sensitive pages on a schedule and show real dates.
- Do not stuff keywords, write for bots, or publish thin AI-generated filler; quality and originality are what differentiate.

## Before/after example
Before: "Our innovative platform leverages cutting-edge AI to empower teams to unlock their potential."
After: "Acme Scheduler is a meeting-scheduling tool for sales teams of 5-200 reps. It routes inbound leads to the right rep in under 60 seconds and integrates with Salesforce and HubSpot. Plans start at $19 per user per month (as of October 2026)."
(Illustrative copy; use the brand's real facts.)

## Content gap briefs
For each lost prompt from the audit, produce:
- Target prompt(s) and engines where the brand is absent
- Page type and working title in the buyer's phrasing
- Direct answer draft (40-80 words)
- Outline with H2 questions
- Data/proof needed and who supplies it
- Schema to add
- Internal links in and out
- Third-party sources to pursue for corroboration
- Owner, effort (S/M/L), review date
